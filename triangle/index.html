Triangle balancer

Most pages treat light, CO₂ and fertilizer as three separate questions. They are one question: light sets the demand, and the other two corners either meet it or they don't. Describe your light, your CO₂ method and your dosing regime; this works out what the light level requires, what your setup actually supplies, which corner is holding the tank back, and which lever to move first. It works in ppm bands and sufficiency — deliberately no grams here; the EI and PPS-Pro pages do the dosing.

How this is calculated 
Light is the driver. Daily light integral at the substrate:
  DLI (mol·m⁻²·day⁻¹) = PAR × photoperiod_h × 0.0036     (3600 s/h ÷ 10⁶ µmol/mol)

PAR band → what the light requires (conventions, SPEC §6 — not chemistry):
  Low       PAR < 25        CO2 3 ppm    NO3 5 ppm/week
  Medium    25 ≤ PAR < 50    CO2 20 ppm   NO3 12 ppm/week
  High      50 ≤ PAR ≤ 100   CO2 30 ppm   NO3 22.5 ppm/week
  Very high PAR > 100       CO2 30 ppm   NO3 22.5 ppm/week   (capped — note 9)
  Required NO3 × density (sparse 0.7 · moderate 1.0 · dense 1.3)
  Required PO4 = NO3 × 3.9/22.5 · K = NO3 × 26/22.5 · Fe = NO3 × 0.30/22.5
  (the EI weekly proportions of SPEC §2)

Sufficiency, per corner:
  s_co2 = min(1, supplied_CO2 ÷ required_CO2) × stability
  s_nut = min(1, supplied_NO3 ÷ required_NO3)
  Limiting corner = the smaller; both ≥ 0.9 → in balance.

Supplied CO2: none 3 ppm · liquid carbon 10 (credit) · DIY yeast 20 × 0.6
stability · pressurized = your target, held at the livestock cap
(hardy 35 ppm · shrimp/sensitive 25 ppm).

Triangle position (a visual, not the calculation):
  iL = min(1, PAR/100) · iC = min(1, CO2_supplied/30) · iN = min(1, NO3_supplied/22.5)
  weights w = i ÷ (iL + iC + iN); point = wL·(0.5, √3/2) + wC·(0, 0) + wN·(1, 0)

Notes

  1. The bands are conventions, not constants of nature. Band edges, required-CO₂ figures and required nutrient bands are the hobby's working conventions — the ranges EI and PPS practice is built around — not measured optima, and PAR at the substrate is itself usually estimated. This page's promise is narrower than the other engines': the comparison (required vs supplied, per corner) is computed consistently and shown in full. Every figure is tabled in SPEC §6 so it can be argued with in one place.
  2. Light-class PAR figures are stand-ins. Low ≈ 20, Medium ≈ 40, High ≈ 80 µmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹ are nominal midpoints for unmeasured fixtures, not data about yours. A measured PAR (or a manufacturer's PAR map for your fixture at your height) beats the class every time.
  3. The 3 ppm ambient figure is a convention. Water in equilibrium with room air holds CO₂ by Henry's law — about 0.6 ppm at 420 ppm air, as the CO₂ page shows — but real tanks sit higher (respiration, imperfect equilibration), and the hobby's working figure is 3 ppm. The same convention is used there and here so the two pages agree.
  4. Liquid carbon is not CO₂. Glutaraldehyde products deliver no measurable CO₂ ppm; their effect on growth is real but partial and works by another route. The 10 ppm "supply" here is a modelling credit for that effect — the softest number on this page — not a measurement.
  5. Stability factors are conventions. DIY yeast is credited at 60% because output sags and swings with temperature and bottle age; liquid carbon at 85%. A pressurized rig on a solenoid is treated as steady. These factors model the swing, not the average.
  6. Livestock caps are safety convention, not toxicology. 35 ppm for none/hardy fish, 25 ppm where shrimp or sensitive stock are kept. If your target sits above the cap, sufficiency is scored at the cap and the page says so. Related convention: on a timer, CO₂ starts about an hour before lights-on so the level is there when the photoperiod starts.
  7. Density multipliers are a heuristic. Sparse 0.7 · moderate 1.0 · dense 1.3 scales the nutrient requirement only — plant mass is the nutrient sink, but light alone doesn't set uptake, and no multiplier here pretends to measure your planting.
  8. Nominal regime figures. Lean bottled (7.5 ppm NO₃/week) is a label-dose stand-in and root tabs (2 ppm) a small water-column credit — tabs feed roots, not the column — because no better source figure exists. EI and PPS-Pro figures are exact from their own pages. "None" counts livestock waste as zero: it is real, and unquantified, so it is stated rather than modelled.
  9. The requirement caps are doctrine. Past ~30 ppm CO₂ and the top of the EI weekly range, more input stops being the answer — the excess is in the light, and the lever list will say so rather than ask for a 40 ppm target.
  10. Excess fertilizer and algae. The oversupply note follows EI doctrine: excess fertilizer alone doesn't grow algae. It is doctrine, printed as doctrine — the flag calls it waste, not a cause.
  11. The plot is a position, not the calculation. The triangle shows where the tank sits among the three corners, from capped intensities. The verdict is computed from the sufficiencies above it, not read off the plot.
